<p class="lead">Picture the Thursday before Mother's Day. You have 38 orders, three of them on the same street, and a customer emailing to ask if the peonies are "in yet." An AI helper sounds great. Then a little voice says: wait. If I paste in my customers' names and addresses, where do they go?</p>

That little voice is right to ask. Here's a plain answer.

## The short answer

Yes, you can. The trick is to use an AI whose **brain runs on your own computer**. Then what you type stays on that computer. Nobody else gets a copy, because there's nowhere else for it to go.

You give up a little. A brain that fits on a normal computer is smaller than the giant ones in big data centers. But for shop work, like replying to a customer or writing a caption, a smaller brain is usually plenty.

## Where your words go when you use AI

Many AI chat tools work like a phone call. You type a question. It travels over the internet to the company's computers. Their computers think up an answer and send it back to you.

That's not sneaky. It's just how a lot of AI works today. But it does mean your words leave your shop. Depending on the company, they might be stored for a while, checked by staff, or used to train future versions. Every company has its own rules, and they usually live in a long privacy policy.

Now picture pasting in "Mrs. Patel, 14 Elm St, allergic to lilies, card says sorry about the dog." That's a lot of someone else's life to send on a trip.

<blockquote class="quote"><p>If a tool can't tell you where your words went, that's your answer.</p></blockquote>

## What "local" means, and what it doesn't

"Local" or "on-device" AI means the brain is a file that sits on your computer. When you ask it something, your own computer does the thinking. Turn off the Wi-Fi and it still works.

Local doesn't always mean nothing ever goes online, though. A local app might still check for updates, download a new brain, or visit a website when you ask it to. That's normal. What matters is that **you can see it**.

That's why we built receipts into Skovern. Every time something leaves your computer, Skovern writes it down: where it went, how big it was, and why. You can read the whole list whenever you like.

## How to check any AI tool in five minutes

You don't need to be a tech person. Grab a coffee and go through these with any tool you're thinking about.

<ol class="how">
  <li><b>Find where the brain runs.</b><span>Look for words like "on-device," "local," or "offline." If the help pages only talk about "our servers," your words go to their servers.</span></li>
  <li><b>Turn off your Wi-Fi and try it.</b><span>If it still answers, the brain is on your computer. If it stops, it was talking to someone else's.</span></li>
  <li><b>Search the privacy policy for "train."</b><span>Look for a clear yes or no on whether your chats are used to improve their AI.</span></li>
  <li><b>Check what gets kept, and for how long.</b><span>A good tool says it plainly, in one place, without a scavenger hunt.</span></li>
  <li><b>Ask how you'd know what it sent.</b><span>If the answer is "trust us," keep looking.</span></li>
</ol>
